The full picture — including data quality

This page shows how the Year One numbers were built and verified. It documents the two data cleanups that affected the prior year — the August 2024 bot spike and Google's 2025 spam filtering — and how the headline metrics hold up across three independent data sources.

Why this page exists

How the numbers were built

To build this dashboard, we pulled data from three independent sources: Google Analytics 4 (the system of record for conversions), Squarespace Analytics (the platform's built-in measurement), and Google Search Console (Google's own search data).

All three sources agree on the direction of Year One's performance. They also corroborate one anomaly in the prior year's data: a single-month spike in August 2024 that wasn't flagged at the time.

Every number on the main dashboard can be traced back to one of these sources.

■Why Year 0 Sessions Looked Higher two converging data cleanups, not lost audience
Data Quality Disclosure

Year 0 traffic was inflated by ~38,000 bot visits plus broader spam Google has since filtered out.

August 2024 stands out in Year 0's monthly traffic. The site received about 38,000 visits that month, against a typical baseline of 4,000 to 10,000 visits. The spike traced back to automated, non-human traffic from Ashburn, Virginia — an area dense with data centers, including Amazon Web Services. None of those visits engaged with the site.

That's the first cleanup. The second is broader: Google's 2025 spam updates filtered low-quality traffic across the entire internet. Direct sessions to loebleadership.com fell from 62,006 to 26,280 — roughly the volume of bot traffic Google removed. Most B2B sites saw similar declines. This was an industry-wide reset, not a Loeb-specific problem.

The key point: none of the removed traffic was converting. Conversions held at 519 in Year 0 and grew to 601 in Year 1 (+16%). The cleanup made the real conversion rate visible — it more than doubled (0.44% → 1.00%, +128%). The audience that remains is engaging more (+46% time on site, +34% engaged sessions per user) and converting at over twice the rate.

i.

The August 2024 spike is visible in Squarespace

The Squarespace traffic chart shows a clear ~38K-visit single-month surge against a 4–10K monthly baseline. No marketing campaign produces this pattern.

ii.

Direct traffic fell almost exactly as expected

Bot traffic typically lands in the "Direct" channel (no referrer). GA4 shows Direct sessions dropped from 62,006 to 26,280 — close to the bot volume Google's spam updates targeted.

iii.

Conversions never came from the removed traffic

If those visits had been real audience, conversions would have fallen alongside sessions. Instead, conversions grew 16% — the removed traffic never produced leads.

■Three Sources, One Story how we know the numbers are right
Cross-Source Verification

Every Year 1 claim is verified by at least two independent data sources

Cross-checking three sources means no number on the main dashboard depends on a single tool's measurement.

Google Analytics 4

System of record for conversions and engagement. Year 1: 63,054 sessions, 601 conversions, 1.00% conversion rate (per user key event rate). Year 0: 116,127 sessions, 519 conversions, 0.44% conversion rate. Both periods are full 12-month equivalents from the same GA4 property.

Squarespace Analytics

Platform-level cross-check. Year 1: 57,184 visits (within 9% of GA4 sessions). Also provides bounce rate, traffic source breakdowns, and page-level data for both years.

Google Search Console

Google's own search data. Year 1 average position 20.7 (matches Squarespace's 20). Year 1: 21,254 clicks, 5.26M impressions (matches Squarespace within 5%).

The honest read

Year One grew quality — and AI conversions grew nearly 5×.

Conversion rate more than doubled (0.44% → 1.00%, +128%). Average engagement time per user grew 46% (28s → 41s). Engaged sessions per user grew 34%. Search clicks grew 63%. Search visibility grew 158%. Average position improved from page 3 to page 2, with page 1 wins on the buyer-intent terms that drive business.

Sessions dropped 46% because Year 0 was inflated. The August 2024 bot spike and Google's 2025 spam filtering removed traffic that was never converting. Direct sessions alone fell from 62,006 to 26,280 — exactly where bot traffic typically lands. The audience that remains is finding Loeb through Google and AI tools, engaging more, and converting at over twice the rate.

AI search became dramatically more productive. ChatGPT and Perplexity sent 2,796 sessions to Loeb's site in Year One — slightly more than Year 0 — and generated 53 leads, up from 11 the year before (+382%). ChatGPT visitors convert at 2.30%, nearly 2× the rate of Google Organic traffic (1.25%). AI didn't appear in Year One; it became one of Loeb's highest-converting channels.

Every claim on the main dashboard is verified across at least two independent sources.
