Website Redesign & Brand Refresh

This proposal outlines two options for the Loeb Leadership website redesign project. The goal is to position Loeb as a leading-edge, modern competitor in the leadership development, training, and coaching space. Both options assume a Q2 2027 launch, with pre-launch phases beginning in 2026.

Prepared forLoeb Leadership Prepared byLexi Gibson · Clarity Communications, LLC DateAugust 2026 Valid throughOctober 31, 2026
01 · Starting Point

Current State

The site has 401 pages, per the August 2026 sitemap: roughly 145 core pages and 255 blog posts. The blog performs well. 78% of posts rank on page one of Google, and the blog is the main source of AI-engine citations, which convert at about twice the rate of organic search. In both options, the full blog migrates as-is.

The 145 core pages need far fewer design decisions than the count suggests. One bio template covers multiple team member pages. One design covers the industry pages. That is why a fixed price is possible on a site this size.

How This Project Works With the Existing Retainer

Copy and design for the homepage, Services pages, and Location pages are delivered under the existing monthly retainer and are not billed within this project. This project covers everything else: site structure, page designs, the site build, address protection, search-ranking protection, and testing, plus copy for pages the retainer does not cover. There is no overlap and no double billing.

02 · The Options

Two Options

Both options are complete projects on their own. They differ in one decision: keep the current Loeb brand, or update it. Option 1 rebuilds the website on the existing brand. Option 2 does the same rebuild on a fully refreshed brand identity, launched together.

A full redesign of the website. The site gets a new structure that leads with programs and outcomes, keeps law firms as the flagship audience, and makes room for other industries to grow. Included

Option 2 · Brand Identity + Website

Rebrand + Reposition

Summary

Everything in Option 1, plus a full update of the Loeb visual identity: logo, colors, fonts, and imagery style. The identity is designed first, and the new website is designed on it from day one. The new brand and the new site launch together, in a single rollout, so everything Loeb puts in front of a client looks like it comes from the same modern company.

Brand Architecture: Consolidating the Sub-Brand Logos

Loeb currently has separate logos for its programs and service lines: the Impact Series marks (Making an Impact, Managing for Impact, Leading for Impact), Real Talk, Leadership Coaching, Organizational Consulting, Learning & Development, and others. Each one competes with the Loeb name for attention, and none is recognized on its own. The recommendation is to retire these logos. They would be replaced with one consistent text treatment under the main Loeb brand. Every program then builds recognition for Loeb instead of splitting it, and future program launches get faster because the naming system already exists.

Included — Everything in Option 1, Plus:

  • Brand workshop with Loeb leadership: what the brand should say, how it should feel, and who it competes with
  • Brand architecture audit and sub-brand consolidation plan (per above)
  • Logo redesign: 3 concepts, refinement of the selected direction, final files in all formats
  • Color system and typography system, selected for digital accessibility
  • Program text treatments replacing the retired sub-brand logos
  • Photography and imagery direction
  • Brand guidelines document (12–18 pages: usage rules, do/don't, file library)
  • A one-page messaging foundation: tagline options, a boilerplate company description, and a short elevator description
  • PowerPoint template: master slides plus 10–15 layouts
  • Core sales collateral on the new identity: services one-pager, email banner set, LinkedIn page assets
  • Rollout support: social profiles, email templates, and the book page updated at launch, so the old and new logos are never in public use at the same time

Post-launch support is already covered under the existing monthly retainer; there is no separate maintenance or support fee after launch for any option.

Build Approach

  • New page designs are built privately on the live site and are not visible to visitors. The current site stays live and unchanged throughout the project.
  • Sitewide style changes (fonts and colors) are applied during a scheduled launch window to keep any visible transition to a few hours.
  • For Option 2, the full rebrand is built on a private duplicate of the site, and the web address is moved to the new site at launch. A test duplicate is run at kickoff to confirm exactly what carries over, and that inventory becomes the launch checklist.
  • Option 2 requires a second Squarespace subscription while the private build site and the current site run side by side (roughly 3–5 months). Loeb pays Squarespace directly for the second subscription at Squarespace's current rates, and the original subscription is canceled after launch.

Scope Changes

Anything not on the included lists above is a scope change. Scope changes are agreed in writing, with an estimate, before work begins, and are billed at $150/hr, consistent with the consulting agreement. Multi-page design assets are priced per the existing design rate card.

07 · Reference

Definitions

Plain-English definitions of the terms used in this proposal.

TermMeaning
Site structure (information architecture)How the site's pages are organized and grouped: what appears in the menus, what lives under what, and how a visitor gets from one area to another.
Page design (template)A reusable page layout. One design can power many pages. All 40+ team bios, for example, use a single design.
Design systemThe set of rules for how the site looks: which fonts, colors, spacing, and image styles are used, and where. It keeps every page consistent.
Brand identityThe visual elements that represent the company everywhere, on and off the website: the logo, colors, fonts, and image style.
Brand identity workCreating new versions of those elements: a new logo, new colors, new fonts. A refresh, by contrast, keeps the current elements and applies them better.
Page address (URL)The web address of a page, e.g. loebleadership.com/coaching.
RedirectAn automatic forward from an old page address to a new one. It keeps saved links, email links, and Google results working after an address changes.
Search-ranking protection (SEO migration)The work of keeping Google rankings through a redesign: keeping or forwarding page addresses, and carrying over the titles, descriptions, and behind-the-scenes code Google reads.
AI citationsWhen AI tools like ChatGPT reference Loeb's pages in their answers. These referrals convert to leads at about twice the rate of Google search.
Call to action (CTA)A button or prompt inviting the visitor to take the next step, such as Contact Us or Download the Guide.
Lead magnetA free resource, such as a guide or checklist, offered in exchange for a visitor's contact information.
Approval gateA checkpoint at the end of each phase where Loeb reviews and signs off before the next phase begins.
Launch testing (QA)Checking every page, link, form, and device size before and after the new site goes live.
