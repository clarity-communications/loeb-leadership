LinkedIn Best Practices

A short guide to getting started on LinkedIn. How often to post, what to post, how to reshare, and what to avoid.

01

How often to post

Two to three posts a week is a good target. Three to five is the upper end. If that seems like a lot, start with one post a week and keep it up for two months. Consistency matters more than volume.

Why more is not better

LinkedIn avoids showing the same person's posts to the same reader twice in a short window. If you post twice in one day, the second post mostly competes with the first. Spacing posts out reaches more people.

Weekday mornings work best for work-related content. The exact time matters less than posting regularly.

02

What to post

Some formats reach more people than others. Ranked, strongest at the top:

  • Best A document or carousel A short PDF that readers swipe through.
  • Strong Short video filmed on your phone Production quality is not required.
  • Strong Photos of real things A workshop, a room, a whiteboard, a team.
  • Good Plain text with a specific observation No image needed.

If you are not sure what to post

Write about one specific thing you noticed this week. A question a client asked. A pattern you keep seeing. Something you changed your mind about. You do not need a conclusion or a lesson.

03

How to reshare a post

Resharing is the quickest way to post without writing from scratch. There are two ways to do it and they work differently.

  1. Find the post you want to share and scroll to the bottom of it.
  2. Look for the reshare icon. It is two arrows forming a loop, next to Comment and labeled Repost.
  3. Click it. A short menu appears with two choices.
  4. Pick one of the two options below.
Option A

Repost

Shares the post to your network exactly as it is, with credit to the original author. You write nothing.

Use this when you want to pass something along and have nothing specific to add.

Option B

Repost with your thoughts

Opens a box for your own commentary. LinkedIn treats this as a new post from you and judges it on its own.

Use this when you have something specific to say.

Commentary length matters

If you choose Repost with your thoughts and write something short like "Great insights," the post reaches fewer people than if you had written nothing at all. Around a hundred words of real commentary performs well.

The rule is simple. If you have a hundred words to add, write them. If you do not, use the plain Repost.

What good commentary looks like

Weak

"Great piece on psychological safety. Couldn't agree more."

Strong

"This matches something I see constantly. In one recent session, a team told me they had never once heard their manager admit to getting something wrong. Not once in four years. When we talked about why people stayed quiet in meetings, that was the whole answer. The behavior that builds safety is not a policy or a workshop. It is a leader saying out loud that they missed something, in front of the people who watched them miss it."

The second one contains something only you could have written: a specific situation, a detail from your own work. That is what gets it distributed.

You can also just comment

Leaving a comment on the original post often does more for it than a silent reshare, and takes less time than writing full commentary.

04

Writing posts

Only the first two lines show before a reader has to click see more. Open with the specific thing rather than the introduction to it.

Weak opening

"I've been thinking a lot lately about leadership development and wanted to share some reflections on a topic that comes up often in my work."

Strong opening

"A managing partner told me last week that she has never given a piece of critical feedback without rehearsing it in the car first."

Keep paragraphs short with space between them, and one idea per paragraph. Write the way you would explain something to a colleague rather than the way you would write a report.

Length is flexible. Anywhere from a short paragraph to around 250 words works. Time spent reading counts in your favor, so do not cut something that is genuinely interesting.

05

After you post

LinkedIn shows a new post to a small group first and watches the response. If that group engages within the first hour, LinkedIn shows the post to more people. If nothing happens, distribution stops.

  • Post when you have time free afterward, not right before a meeting.
  • Reply to every comment, ideally within an hour or two.
  • Write a real reply rather than a reaction. A reply counts as a comment.
  • Spend ten minutes commenting on other people's posts before publishing your own.
06

What to avoid

  • Lots of hashtags. Zero to three is enough. LinkedIn reads the words in your post to work out the topic.
  • Engagement bait. "Like if you agree" and "comment YES below" are filtered down.
  • Posting the same text everywhere. A post written for LinkedIn reads differently from one written for another platform.
  • Asking colleagues to like your post. Coordinated engagement is detected and discounted.
  • Selling in most of your posts. Roughly four useful posts for every promotional one.
07

Your first month

A simple way to start.

  • Week one. Reshare two posts you found useful. Use the plain repost and write nothing.
  • Week two. Leave a comment on three posts.
  • Week three. Write one short post about something specific from your work.
  • Week four. Post again and reply to anyone who responds.

Reflects LinkedIn platform behavior as of July 2026. The platform changes often, so treat the specifics as current rather than permanent.
