What the Numbers Mean

Position 4.86 on a page that's been live for 8 weeks is genuinely strong. Most new pages take 3-6 months to reach page 1. The book page is already there for branded and near-branded queries. The low impression volume reflects the niche query pool for a newly launched legal industry book — not a ranking failure. As awareness grows, impressions will follow.

Constraints
1
Limited publisher support
PLI Press is a legal education publisher, not a trade book marketer. The launch was handled without sufficient advance notice to plan a pre-order campaign. Podcast outreach, media relations, and proactive retail distribution — standard for trade book launches — have not materialized. Loeb's owned channels are carrying the promotional weight.
2
Paperback only — no ebook or audiobook
No digital formats exist yet. This blocks the entire modern book marketing funnel. Ebooks enable impulse purchases from social and email. Audiobooks reach the legal professional commute audience. Without them, every interested reader hits a checkout process that requires waiting for physical delivery — and most won't complete it.
3
Price point creates purchase friction
PLI prices books for a professional legal reference market. Mainstream leadership books — Brené Brown's Strong Ground, Adam Grant's titles — sell for $25–35 hardcover and $10–15 ebook, shipped free or downloaded instantly. Beyond the Courtroom carries a higher price plus ~$12 shipping through PLI. That gap kills impulse purchases regardless of how good the marketing is.
Priority Ask for PLI

Ebook and audiobook formats are the single most impactful thing PLI can do for this book. Until they exist, all LinkedIn ad campaigns will drive to a free chapter download — not a purchase link. The chapter download captures email, demonstrates value, and creates a warm lead for eventual purchase. This is the correct approach given current format and pricing constraints.

01
The Big 4 are contracting — and leaving clients behind
Deloitte, PwC, EY, and KPMG have cut thousands of consulting roles since 2023. PwC eliminated nearly 3,300 U.S. positions in 2024–2025 alone — its first major reduction since 2009. Consulting revenue is declining at several firms.

The opportunity: Organizations that relied on large consulting firms for leadership development, coaching, and organizational strategy now need a credible alternative. Loeb is that alternative — expert, relational, and built for exactly the work the Big 4 are stepping back from.
High priority · position now
02
AI search is reshaping how buyers find firms like Loeb
Decision-makers are asking ChatGPT and Perplexity for firm recommendations before Googling. Loeb is already being cited — and AI visitors are converting at 2.30%, nearly twice Google's rate.

The window: Most competitors don't know this channel exists. The time to establish AI search authority is now.
Loeb already ahead

Most L&D investment goes to senior leaders. Front-line and mid-level managers — who have the greatest day-to-day impact on retention and team performance — are chronically under-developed.

The opportunity: Content and programs targeting this audience address a real market gap and signal Loeb's full-pipeline capability to buyers.

Loeb vs. Large Consulting Firms · The Positioning
The Case for Choosing Loeb

Large consulting firms offer leadership development at scale. But scale is the problem. Programs are process-heavy, leader-agnostic, and expensive to adapt. Clients get frameworks, not relationships.

Loeb offers something different: leadership development built around the actual humans doing the leading. Coaching relationships that span years. Executive voices who have led organizations and coached thousands of leaders. A team that knows your industry, your culture, and your people.

As the Big 4 pull back from this work, Loeb should be the firm that steps forward — through content, through thought leadership, and through Results Snapshots that demonstrate outcomes without requiring formal case studies.

Results Snapshots are 2-3 sentence outcomes statements — anonymous, general, drawn from real work — embedded in blog posts and LinkedIn content. They demonstrate impact without the approval process or production lift of a formal case study. Example: "Organizations that have worked with Loeb on M&A integration have seen measurable reductions in voluntary turnover in the 90 days post-close."

Reporting Cadence

Metrics will be monitored on an ongoing basis and reported out quarterly to the Loeb Leadership team.

Conversion Definition

A conversion (MQL) is a website visitor who completes a meaningful contact action — form submission, consultation request, or gated content download. It excludes newsletter signups (too low-intent) and closed engagements (outside marketing's control).

This matches the MQL-grade definition used by Ruler Analytics, the source for the 2.9% B2B median benchmark — so comparisons are apples to apples.

Data Sources

Primary: Google Analytics 4 — conversions, sessions, source/medium, engagement.

Search: Google Search Console — clicks, impressions, position, page-level data.

Social: LinkedIn analytics export — impressions, engagement, follower growth.

Cross-check: Squarespace Analytics — independent traffic and search validation.

The Year Two Commitment

Every target in this strategy is grounded in verified data. Every claim can be sourced. That standard carries forward — performance is tracked quarterly and reported annually with the same transparency as Year One.
