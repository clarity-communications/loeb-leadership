A simple way to think about it

The restaurant that changed neighborhoods

Picture a restaurant on a busy tourist street. A thousand people walk past every day. But only ten come inside — and most of them just glance at the menu and leave.

Now picture the same restaurant moves to a quieter neighborhood. Only four hundred people walk by. But eight of them come in, sit down, and order dinner.

That's what happened to Loeb's website. Fewer visitors than last year — but the right ones. They find Loeb through Google, they're actually interested, and they're twice as likely to become leads.

What this means in plain language

Times Shown is how often a Loeb page came up in Google search results. Going from 1.9M to 4.9M means Google now treats Loeb's content as relevant for 2.6× more searches.

Average Search Rank is where Loeb's pages typically land on Google. Rank 29 meant showing up near the bottom of page 3, where almost no one clicks. Rank 20 means top of page 2 — with some pages now on page 1. That's the difference between invisible and findable.

Why this matters · The plain English version

The AI channel didn't get much bigger this year. It got much better.

What's happening. Instead of Googling "best leadership consulting firm in NJ," people increasingly ask ChatGPT or Perplexity. Those tools answer by recommending specific firms. Being one of the recommended firms is the new version of being on page 1 of Google.

What we found. AI sent Loeb about 2,800 visits this year — only slightly more than last year's 2,500. But those visits produced 53 leads, up from just 11. Same channel, roughly the same traffic, nearly five times the leads.

Why those visitors convert so well. When ChatGPT recommends Loeb, the visitor already trusts the recommendation. They're not comparison-shopping anymore. That's why they convert at 2.30% — nearly double Google's 1.21%.

One shift worth noting. The type of AI traffic changed. Browser referral clicks from ChatGPT actually went down (2,002 to 1,676), but visits from inside the ChatGPT app itself grew into a real segment (587 visits, 14 leads) — one that barely existed a year ago. That in-app behavior is the clearest signal that people are genuinely using AI assistants to find firms like Loeb.

The opportunity for next year. Most consulting firms don't know AI tools are sending them traffic at all. Loeb has a head start, a verified baseline to grow from, and the right kind of content — FAQ schema, expert authorship, structured posts — that AI tools prefer. Next year is about deliberately growing a channel we've now proven converts.

Why total traffic looks smaller this year — and why that's a good thing

Total visits dropped from 116,127 last year to 63,054 this year. That's not lost audience — it's cleanup.

Two things happened. First, a single weird spike of ~38,000 visits in August 2024 turned out to be automated bot traffic from a Virginia data center. None of it was real people. Second, Google's 2025 spam updates filtered out junk traffic across the whole internet. Most sites saw their numbers drop. Loeb included.

Here's how we know this is good news. Visits dropped 46%, but leads went up 16%. Conversion rate more than doubled. Time on site jumped 46%. Bounce rate improved 7 points. Less traffic, but the traffic that's left is engaged and converting.
